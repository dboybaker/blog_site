## Intro

It is abundantly clear we've entered the age of hyper-specific inference engines tuned to a specific model and specific hardware. I was scrolling Reddit when I came across a post regarding Ninfer6000, a fork of Ninfer specifically tuned for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next NVFP4 on RTX 6000 Blackwell. The reported speed numbers were incredible, so I HAD to grab it and benchmark it. If it really was going to bring me from ~150tok/s decode (vLLM + MTP) to well over 300, I needed to see it. More importantly I needed to see if it was lobotomized to achieve those speeds. To accomplish this I've run a host of traditional benchmarks to log the speed, size, accuracy, and long context behavior. I've also run a couple in-harness tests to compare how the models behave.

## The Setup

I used llama-swap to run each one at a time through [evalscope](https://evalscope.readthedocs.io/) 1.8.1 for the scored suites, and a direct runner for RULER. I set concurrency 2 for all runs except one long context RULER test because that fit nicely into my hardware.

| | Ninfer6000 | vLLM | llama.cpp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checkpoint | Swift 1.5 NVFP4 + FP8 PLE/Proj | NVFP4 (`modelopt`) | UD-Q4_K_XL |
| KV cache | BF16 | BF16 | Q8_0 |
| Context served | 262,144 | 262,144 | 131,072 (2 streams @ 262k) |
| Speculative decode | MTP, 3 draft tokens | MTP, 2 tokens | MTP, 3 draft tokens |
| Max concurrent requests | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Prefill / ubatch chunk | 8,192 | 8,192 | 2,048 |

UD-Q4_K_XL's 131,072-token context is the one asymmetry amongst the trio. The two streams share the single 262,144 token pool in llama.cpp, but none of the tests came near this so the comparisons are still fair in that regard. The deep RULER ladder is the exception. To hit the 256,827 token prompt I dropped it to a single 262,144 token context stream for that benchmark.

Ninfer6000's model is not perfectly apples to apples to the other two as it is based on a Swift1.5 fine tune geared toward reasoning brevity. That being said, it is the model Ninfer6000 recommends so it stands as how the engine is to be configured.

I first ran a greedy pass with a low max-token cap, then 3 repeats at `temp 0.6` and a budget of 16k tokens. Everything going forward refers to the 3-repeat run unless the section says otherwise.

## Speed & Size

### The sweep: prefill, decode, time-to-first-token

First order of business was verifying the speed difference, with a note on RAM occupied. I measured the prefill, decode, and TTFT (time to first token) one engine at a time, **single stream** (no concurrency), the same synthetic prompt on all three, five prompt lengths from ~1k to 114k tokens, 512 tokens out, greedy, two reps per cell. I pulled the memory and load numbers from the logs. Prefill at 1k was noisy on each model (vLLM's two reps were 2.3x apart for example), so treat sub-8k prefill as directional.

#### Decode: flat, and ~1.5-1.7x vLLM at every length

<img src="speed_decode_stream.png">

| Prompt | Ninfer6000 | vLLM nvfp4 | llama.cpp Q4_K_XL | N vs vLLM | N vs llama.cpp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~1k | 232 | 159 | 138 | 1.46x | 1.68x |
| 7.2k | 241 | 148 | 129 | 1.63x | 1.87x |
| 28.6k | 227 | 150 | 112 | 1.51x | 2.02x |
| 57.1k | 249 | 149 | 93 | 1.68x | 2.67x |
| 114.1k | 240 | 144 | 65 | 1.66x | 3.70x |
| **mean** | **238** | 150 | 108 | **1.59x** | **2.21x** |

The most significant number here is presented by the purple line: Ninfer6000 averages 238 tok/s, stays consistent at any prompt size (unlike llama.cpp, though I'm sure this will be fixed at some point), and beats vLLM by ~60%. This says nothing of quality/accuracy of course but Ninfer6000's advertised decode superiority checks out.

#### Prefill and TTFT: vLLM wins this one, decisively

<img src="speed_ttft.png">

| Prompt | Ninfer6000 prefill | vLLM prefill | llama.cpp prefill | Ninfer TTFT | vLLM TTFT | llama.cpp TTFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~1k | 4,315 | 6,944 | 1,823 | 0.23 s | 0.17 s | 0.56 s |
| 7.2k | 7,996 | 16,484 | 2,960 | 0.90 s | 0.44 s | 2.44 s |
| 28.6k | 9,521 | 16,798 | 3,320 | 3.00 s | 1.70 s | 8.61 s |

vLLM is still the prefill champ: ~16.5k tok/s, flat from 8k to 114k, **1.6-2.1x Ninfer6000**. The result is a first token that comes a few seconds earlier. 7.0 s versus 11.4 s on a 114k prompt. Ninfer6000 plateaus at ~10k tok/s, but only finds its stride in the longer prompts. llama.cpp never gets near either, capped at ~3.3k tok/s with a 36.7 second TTFT at 114k.

Quick note on the 1k row: vLLM's two reps were 2.3x apart, attributable to cold-graph/prefix-cache noise. Not very valuable, better to focus on the 8k+ columns as the real prefill comparison.


#### End to end: the crossover is output length, not context length

If you consider a real-world use case, both prefill and decode impact the outcome notably. Wall time per request = measured TTFT + (output tokens / decode rate):

| Prompt | Output | Ninfer6000 | vLLM nvfp4 | llama.cpp Q4_K_XL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.2k | 512 | 3.0 s | 3.9 s | 6.4 s |
| 7.2k | 2,048 | **9.4 s** | 14.2 s | 18.3 s |
| 28.6k | 2,048 | **12.0 s** | 15.3 s | 26.9 s |
| 57.1k | 8,192 | **38.6 s** | 58.5 s | 105.4 s |
| 114.1k | 512 | 13.5 s | **10.6 s** | 44.6 s |
| 114.1k | 2,048 | **19.9 s** | 21.2 s | 68.3 s |
| 114.1k | 8,192 | **45.5 s** | 63.9 s | 162.9 s |

Setting the two projections equal gives the crossover - the output length at which Ninfer6000's decode lead repays its prefill debt:

| Prompt | ~1k | 7.2k | 28.6k | 57.1k | 114.1k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crossover, output tokens | 31 | 179 | 580 | 863 | 1,567 |

So the nuanced conclusion of which is better would be "it depends". Ask the model for a one-line answer on a huge prompt and vLLM cooks. Ask it for anything a human would call an answer (a paragraph, a diff, a tool trace) and Ninfer6000 wins at every prompt length, and the margin grows (1.4x faster end-to-end at 114k prompt / 8k output). Chat and agent traffic often sits on the long-output side of that line, so I'm personally inclined to lean toward the decode leader, but I also acknowledge prefill wins can be more perceivable to the end user in chat after a certain decode speed. llama.cpp is on the wrong side of both: 163 seconds for that same 114k/8k request. Oof.

### Cold start, VRAM, and where the memory goes

<img src="speed_load.png">

<img src="speed_memory.png">

| | Ninfer6000 | vLLM nvfp4 | llama.cpp Q4_K_XL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artifact | `swift_1_5` `_nvfp4` `_fp8ple` `_fp8proj.ninfer` | `nvfp4` (modelopt) | `UD-Q4_K_XL` + `mmproj-BF16` + MTP draft `Q8_0` |
| Time to first ready request | **23 s** | 290 s | 67 s |
| VRAM after load | **86.8 GiB** | 90.3 GiB | 93.7 GiB |
| VRAM peak (131k prompt) | **86.8 GiB** | 92.8 GiB | 94.4 GiB |
| Host RAM peak | 22.2 GiB | 70.1 GiB | 14.6 GiB |
| KV cache dtype | BF16 | BF16 | Q8_0 |

Three points of intrigue here.

**First observation, all three fit on one 96 GB card.**

**Second, vLLM's 290-second boot is a big price for that prefill lead.** I do a lot of experimenting and model swapping on this rig, so startup time actually matters to me. It would be nice to have a whole machine dedicated to high availability vLLM multi-user serving, but this is real life. Ninfer6000 is serving traffic 23 seconds after a request cold-start and has probably finished its response by the time nvfp4 has even begun to prefill.

**Third, memory fill varies greatly.** vLLM sits at 70 GiB of RAM because I ran it with `VLLM_PLE_CPU_OFFLOAD=1`. The n-gram table state lives in system memory and streams in, which is also likely part of where that prefill efficiency comes from. llama.cpp memory-maps the GGUF and lets the page cache deal with the rest. Ninfer6000 actually uses the least VRAM of the three.

## Conclusion

{{< verdict >}}
<p class="verdict-lead">tl;dr</p>

- The speed benefit is real. The massive decode gap makes tasks extremely rapid. Prefill falls short of vLLM but still WAY faster than llama.cpp.
- Benchmarks are mostly a wash with the one surprising inverted result of Ninfer6000 topping BFCL and failing tool calls in real world applications consistently.
- The knowledge and accuracy differences didn't move beyond the noise floor in most spots and suggest the models are interchangeable for my needs. 

If the tool calling issue is solved, Ninfer6000 will be the obvious choice for me. Until then I'll eat the vLLM startup time/decode reduction and benefit from prefill gains (even though total wall time will be worse).
